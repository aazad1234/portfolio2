The process

No mystery, no black box.

A simple rhythm that keeps decisions visible and the work moving.

  1. 01

    Understand

    A short conversation and a written brief. We agree on the problem, the audience, the constraints, and what success looks like.

  2. 02

    Shape

    Sketch the structure, map the content, and test the riskiest assumptions before committing to a full build.

  3. 03

    Build

    Work in small, reviewable increments with accessible components, clear data flow, and no surprise rewrites.

  4. 04

    Hand over

    Ship with documentation, a maintainable codebase, and a measured list of what to improve next.

Good questions

Before we start.

A few honest answers to the things people usually ask first.

How do projects usually begin?

With a short conversation and a written brief. I ask about the goal, the audience, the constraints, and what has already been tried. If the fit is right, I propose a small first phase with a clear deliverable.

Do you work with existing codebases?

Yes. I start by reading the code and the product, not by rewriting it. The first step is usually a short review that identifies what to keep, what to clarify, and what to change.

Can you work with a designer or product team?

That is often the best setup. I can implement an existing design system, pair with a designer on interaction details, or take a product problem from sketch to production.

How do you handle scope and changes?

Scope is written down, and changes are discussed before they become work. If a new idea is valuable, we trade it against something else or agree to a separate phase. Quiet scope growth is not good for anyone.

What do you need from me?

A brief, access to the current product or code, and a person who can make decisions. The rest we can figure out together.
