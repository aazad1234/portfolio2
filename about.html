My story

Typographic sensitivity, then relentless craft.

I started out screenprinting experimental posters and setting metal lead type, and never lost that love for the weight of ink on paper.

Over the past eight years, that fascination evolved into an art direction practice spanning brand identity systems, publication design, bespoke grotesk typefaces, and sustainable packaging. Rooted in Swiss modernist principles, I believe great design communicates with quiet conviction rather than superficial ornament.

I collaborate closely with independent founders, publishers, and cultural institutions, translating conceptual brand ethos into tactile objects and cohesive digital design languages.

Outside client commissions, I continually experiment with variable font axes in Glyphs, research zero-waste bio-substrates, and collect mid-century European monographs.

How I work

Principles I keep coming back to.

Not slogans, just the habits that tend to make projects calmer and better.

01

Start with the problem

Understand the person, the constraint, and the outcome before choosing the stack or the layout. The brief is part of the design.

02

Make it usable by default

Keyboard access, readable contrast, honest labels, and sensible focus states are not polish. They are the baseline.

03

Leave it maintainable

Clear names, contained components, and documentation where the next decision will need it. Future-you is a real user too.

04

Measure, then improve

Performance work should start from evidence. Real devices and real network conditions beat assumptions every time.

05

Communicate the trade-offs

Every project has constraints. Saying them out loud early is kinder than discovering them late, and it makes better decisions possible.

06

Keep learning in public

Write down what worked, what did not, and what changed. The notes become a better starting point for the next build.
